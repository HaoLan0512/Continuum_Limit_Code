                                1. Introduction
   Moiré materials are formed by stacking two-dimensional crystalline layers with
a small relative twist angle or lattice mismatch. The superposition of the lattice
periodicities produces a long-wavelength moiré pattern whose length scale can be
much larger than the lattice spacing of either layer. Moiré materials have emerged
as an ideal experimental platform for investigating strongly correlated electronic
phases. Notable examples include superconductivity in magic-angle twisted bilayer
graphene [1] and the fractional quantum anomalous Hall effect in twisted bilayer
MoTe2 [10].
   Mechanical relaxation is the process by which atoms in moiré materials move
from their monolayer equilibrium positions in order to minimize the total energy of
the bilayer. This relaxation is governed by a competition between the elastic cost of
deforming each layer and the dependence of the interlayer energy on local stacking.
When the twist angle or lattice mismatch is small, this competition tends to enlarge
regions of energetically favorable stacking and localize the transitions between them
to narrow domain walls [9, 2, 3]. Atomic and electronic reconstruction for relaxation
have been observed experimentally [13]. Together with calculations showing that
relaxation can substantially modify the low-energy electronic band structure [9],
these observations indicate that accurate prediction of moiré materials’ electronic
properties requires reliable modeling of the relaxed atomic structure.
   A variety of approaches have therefore been used to determine relaxed structures,
including density-functional theory calculations [11], molecular dynamics [12], mul-
tiscale modeling [6], and effective continuum theories [9, 2]. Common continuum
models involve minimizing an energy that balances intralayer elasticity against an
interlayer misfit energy determined by local stacking, using continuous displace-
ment or modulation fields defined in real or configuration space [6, 9, 2, 4, 3].
Despite the widespread use of such continuum models, their precise relation to
the underlying atomistic problem remains incompletely understood. In particular,
Cazeaux, Luskin, and Massatt [4] discuss the question of whether solutions of their
continuum elastostatic model are close to solutions of the atomistic problem that it
approximates. This atomistic-to-continuum question motivates the present work.
   To address this question, we study a one-dimensional minimal model. The main
contribution of the present work is a microscopic derivation of the associated con-
tinuum relaxation functional, together with a quantitative atomistic-to-continuum
convergence result. Specifically, we consider two periodic chains with common pe-
riod 2π, containing N and N + 1 atoms per period, respectively, so that the lattice
mismatch tends to zero as N → ∞. After rescaling, the atomistic energy con-
sists of harmonic nearest-neighbor intralayer terms and an interlayer interaction
